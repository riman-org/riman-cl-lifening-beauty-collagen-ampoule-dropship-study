\begin{abstract}
Background: The introduction of oral nutraceuticals into the Latin American market requires rigorous validation of bioavailability claims against local regulatory frameworks and consumer sensory preferences. This study evaluates the LIFENING Beauty Collagen Ampoule Drink (containing MFDS-certified Collagen Peptide SH 2,000mg) within the Chilean context, focusing on a zero-inventory drop-shipping model for independent planners. Methods: A stratified survey of N=200 participants across Santiago, Valparaiso, and Concepcion assessed Likert-scale perceptions of formulation efficacy, packaging stability, and regulatory confidence, alongside Net Promoter Score (NPS) metrics. Results: Overall composite mean was 3.29 with a Top-2-Box percentage of 41.4\%. Key findings indicate strong trust in MFDS certification (Mean 3.83, T2B 76.0\%) and ingredient alignment with dermatological trends (Mean 4.04, T2B 85.0\%). However, logistical latency concerns dominate barriers to adoption (71.0\%), and the liquid ampoule format received low suitability scores for dry climates (Mean 2.2, T2B 17.5\%). The NPS score was -19.5, driven by high passive sentiment (73.5\%) and detractor rates (23.0\%). Strategic Implications: While product quality is perceived as competitive against K-Beauty importers (73.5\% benchmark), success depends on mitigating cold-chain integrity risks and clarifying SEREMI labeling compliance. Automated logistics support is critical for planner retention.

\vspace{0.4em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} LIFENING Beauty Collagen Ampoule, Chile Nutraceutical Regulation, Zero-Inventory Drop-Shipping, Collagen Peptide SH, SEREMI Compliance, Microclimate Adaptation
\end{abstract}

\section{1. Formulation Architecture \& Study Methodology}
The LIFENING Beauty Collagen Ampoule Drink utilizes a patented formulation centered on MFDS-certified Collagen Peptide SH (2,000mg), supplemented by Centella Asiatica, Hyaluronic Acid, Elastin, Biotin, Vitamin C, Glutathione, and Selenium. This study employed a stratified sampling method targeting N=200 consumers and B2B planners in three distinct Chilean microclimates: Santiago (Mediterranean Dry, 36\% RH), Valparaiso (Coastal Cool, 65\% RH), and Concepcion (Oceanic, 68\% RH). The primary objective was to evaluate the feasibility of a 'No Auto-Ship' planner cold-start path using a zero-inventory drop-shipping model. Data collection utilized Likert-5 scales for perceptual attributes and single/multi-choice questions for behavioral intent and regulatory concerns. The methodology specifically tested whether the IR52 Jang Young Sil Award serves as a sufficient quality benchmark for independent recommendation.
\section{2. Quantitative Metrics \& Microclimate Adaptation}
Quantitative analysis reveals a dichotomy between ingredient trust and format suitability. The inclusion of Centella Asiatica and Hyaluronic Acid aligned strongly with current dermatological trends preferred by Chilean consumers, achieving a mean score of 4.04 (Top-2-Box 85.0\%). Similarly, the MFDS certification of Collagen Peptide SH significantly increased trust in bioavailability claims (Mean 3.83, T2B 76.0\%). Conversely, the liquid ampoule format demonstrated poor adaptation to the dry, high-altitude conditions typical of Santiago, with a low mean of 2.2 (T2B 17.5\%). Participants expressed skepticism regarding the taste profile without masking agents (Mean 3.33, T2B 34.5\%). However, the single-dose packaging was highly valued for preventing oxidation issues in humid coastal microclimates like Valparaiso and Concepcion (Mean 3.68, T2B 66.0\%). The overall composite mean stood at 3.29, indicating moderate but cautious acceptance.
\section{3. Cross-Demographic, MBTI \& B2B/C-End Stratification}
Stratification data highlights significant variance across demographic segments. Affluent/High Net Worth individuals reported the highest satisfaction (Mean 3.5), while Lower-Middle Income groups scored lowest (Mean 3.11). By occupation, Cross-Border E-Commerce sellers (B2B) showed higher engagement (Mean 3.4) compared to Software Engineers (C-End, Mean 3.15). MBTI analysis indicates that intuitive types (INTP, INTJ) were more skeptical of the drop-ship model due to mechanism obsession, whereas sensing types (ISFJ, ESFJ) prioritized non-irritating daily comfort. Regarding business models, 71.0\% of respondents cited 'logistics\_latency\_concern' as the primary barrier to adopting zero-inventory drop-shipping. Only 43.0\% viewed 'zero\_capital\_risk' as a viable factor, while 'high\_margin\_structure' (91.0\%) and 'automated\_logistics' (87.5\%) were deemed essential for cold-start viability. Most planners expect to sell 6-15 initial trial units (83.0\%) before committing to broader networks.
\section{4. Regulatory Compliance \& Regional Strategy}
Regulatory confidence remains a critical bottleneck. Only 0.5\% of respondents strongly agreed that the drop-shipping model complies with Chilean SEREMI health regulations for imported nutraceuticals (Mean 2.77, T2B 0.5\%). 'SEREMI\_labeling\_compliance' was selected by 100\% of participants as a key concern, followed by 'customs\_clearance\_delay' (59.5\%) and 'cold\_chain\_integrity' (58.5\%). Competitive positioning primarily benchmarks against K-Beauty importers (73.5\%), rather than local pharmacy brands like Skinceuticals or La Roche-Posay. The Net Promoter Score (NPS) of -19.5 reflects a market dominated by passives (73.5\%) and detractors (23.0\%), suggesting that while product quality is recognized, operational friction undermines advocacy. Strategic recommendations include providing explicit SEREMI-compliant labeling templates, ensuring transparent tax inclusion, and leveraging automated logistics partners to mitigate latency fears. The IR52 award alone is insufficient for recommendation (Mean 3.12, T2B 14.0\%), necessitating stronger clinical data communication.

\section{Conclusion}
This study confirms that while the LIFENING Beauty Collagen Ampoule possesses strong ingredient credibility through MFDS certification and trend-aligned formulations (Centella/Hyaluronic Acid), its market penetration in Chile via a zero-inventory drop-shipping model faces substantial operational and regulatory headwinds. The negative NPS (-19.5) and low confidence in SEREMI compliance highlight that logistical latency and labeling ambiguity are the primary deterrents for both B2B planners and C-end consumers. Success requires shifting focus from purely marketing the 'K-Beauty' origin to demonstrating robust supply chain integrity and regulatory transparency. Implementing automated logistics solutions and providing clear, pre-approved SEREMI labeling documentation are imperative steps to convert passive interest into active advocacy, particularly in humid coastal zones where packaging stability is valued over format convenience in dry inland cities.
